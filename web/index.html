01 Selected work

2026 · iPhone · Game

Scuttle

Pick a boat, drop it in the sea, and see what it takes to sink it. A pixel-art physics sandbox with over 40 hulls that flood, burn, split and capsize. Landscape only, so the sea gets the whole screen.

Swift and SwiftUI, with water and sprites drawn on the GPU in Metal and rigid body physics from Box2D. The simulation is its own tested Swift package. No network, no accounts, no analytics.

App Store

2026 · iPhone and Apple Watch · Founder

Gemel

A calorie and protein tracker with zero AI in it, on purpose. No food database, no barcodes, no feed. You type a number and Gemel paces it across your day, so you know you're off track while there's still time to fix it. Pair with a friend for a streak that only grows on days you both hit your goals.

My first native iOS app. SwiftUI throughout, with widgets, a watchOS app and complications, Siri, Apple Health and StoreKit. SQLite via GRDB, and CloudKit sharing for pairing, so there are no accounts.

App Store getgemel.com

2014 → 2026 · Web and iPhone · Founder

RentChek

Property management for landlords: rent, receipts and expenses across every property. I built it in 2014 to manage my own rentals and I've run it ever since.

The original is AngularJS, NodeJS and MongoDB. In 2026 I rebuilt the front end as one React Native and Expo codebase in TypeScript that ships to both iPhone and the web, on the same API that has run since 2014.

03 About

It started with a Pentium II and RPG Maker. At 16 I released my first game, Time Trigger.

Hosting Counter-Strike servers led to building our clan's website, and that led to noticing websites were becoming applications. I've been building them professionally since 2007.

Along the way I've learned to value boring, well tested code, careful rollouts with a way back, and a local setup any new developer can run in minutes. Post-mortems taught me more than launches did.

These days that includes native iOS, which is how Gemel, Scuttle and the RentChek app came to be. Outside my home office, my wife and our two kids keep me grounded and are the reason I keep trying to be better at home and at work.
